What you will do

Own the frontend (React specialty)
** Build and maintain the public site: content and video pages, watch along and event pages, campaign and sponsorship landing pages.
** Ship features end to end in React and TypeScript: implementation, responsive behaviour, empty and error states, release, and the bug reports that follow.
** Keep it fast. Core Web Vitals, code splitting, image and poster optimisation, caching and CDN behaviour, and sane performance on mid range Android phones and slow networks.
** Look after discovery basics for a media property: metadata, structured data, sitemaps, crawlability, share previews.
** Maintain and extend the internal content management system used by creators and admins: upload, metadata, scheduling, moderation, publishing workflows.
** Build the interfaces that make heavy daily work fast, including tables, filters, bulk actions, drag and drop, media pickers, and upload states that survive a dropped connection.
** Treat internal users as real users. If someone on the content team is losing ten minutes a day to a bad screen, fixing that is real work.

Work across the stack
** Read and contribute to our Go microservices: understand service boundaries, write or modify handlers and endpoints, and ship small backend changes when the fastest fix for a frontend problem lives on the other side of the API.
** Consume APIs across our microservices, and agree contracts early rather than discovering them in production.
** Keep the client typed and honest: generated or hand written API types, no silent any, no assumptions about fields that may not arrive.
** Understand our containers: how a Go service and a React build each get packaged with Docker, how they run in Kubernetes, and what a bad image or a missing environment variable actually breaks.
** Build for degradation: timeouts, retries, loading and error states, feature flags for risky releases, and a page that fails gracefully when one service is unhealthy.
** Handle auth carefully: sessions, tokens, refresh flows, CORS, and the security basics that come with them.

Keep it up
** Own uptime for the web surfaces alongside the backend team: monitoring, alerting, error tracking, uptime checks, and a dashboard someone can actually read at 2am.
** Own the deploy path: pipeline configuration, preview builds, staged rollouts, and a rollback you have practised before you needed it.
** Triage incidents properly. Reproduce, isolate whether the fault is in the frontend, the gateway, or a backend service, escalate with evidence rather than a screenshot, and write up what happened afterwards.

Partner with UI/UX
** Review Figma files early and flag what will be expensive or janky before it is designed rather than after.
** Implement a high level of visual fidelity: spacing, type scale, motion, hover and focus states.
** Grow and maintain a shared component library so the site and other system stay coherent as they expand.
** Build accessibly by default: semantic markup, keyboard navigation, focus management, sensible contrast.

Use AI tooling as a genuine multiplier

** Exercise judgment about what to accept, what to rewrite, and what to reject, and be able to explain why.

Role: AI Engineer Intern, applied NLP, speech and LLM systems 
Team: AI R&D, working directly with the core team

Stipend: Rs. 25,000 to 35,000 per month, based on demonstrated skill 
Conversion: Strong performers receive a PPO for a full time role

The role
You will build the AI systems behind CommBox's content and match day products: pipelines that turn hours of talk into structured, searchable material, language models applied to cricket conversation and cricket data, and the services that put those capabilities in front of real fans.
A note on secrecy: a good part of our AI roadmap has not shipped publicly, so this posting stays deliberately vague about what exactly you will build. You will sign an NDA and see the full picture in week one. If the phrase "LLMs applied to cricket" already has your mind racing, we should talk.

What you will do

Build speech and language pipelines
** Run ASR on long form, multi speaker video and audio: transcription, speaker diarization, word level alignment, and the messy reality of Hinglish and cricket vocabulary.
** Post process transcripts with LLMs: cleaning, segmentation, structuring, and extracting what matters from an hour of conversation.
** Measure quality like you mean it. WER is a start, not an answer. Build test sets, categorize failures, and catch regressions before users do.

Apply LLMs to real products
** Design prompted and fine tuned LLM workflows for extraction, classification, summarization and generation over sports content and structured data.
** Build retrieval systems: embeddings, vector search, chunking that respects the material, and grounding so user facing answers stay honest.
** Put natural language on top of structured data with validation and guardrails, because a confident wrong answer about cricket gets noticed within seconds.

Ship them as production services
** Package pipelines as FastAPI microservices, containerize them, and deploy to Kubernetes through GitLab CI.
** Handle heavy media workloads properly: job queues, batch processing, GPU vs CPU tradeoffs, and cost and latency budgets that someone has to actually meet.
** Instrument everything: logs, traces, quality dashboards. When output degrades, you should be able to tell whether the model, the data or the infra moved.

Work with real data
** Model and store media and sports data in Postgres and Redis, and keep evaluation datasets versioned and trustworthy.
** Run annotation loops: design labeling tasks, measure agreement, and feed the results back into the system instead of into a folder nobody opens.

Use AI tooling as a genuine multiplier
** Use the provided tools like Claude Code and Codex daily for implementation, refactors, migrations, tests and review.
** Exercise judgment about what to accept, what to rewrite and what to reject, and be able to explain why.

What we are looking for

Must have
** Strong Python & package management with UV. Most of your day is Python, and it shows quickly when it is not a real strength.
** At least one NLP or ML project you can walk us through end to end: what you built, what broke, what you measured, what you would do differently. A repo, a demo or a deployed service is perfect. Course certificates alone are not.
** Hands-on experience with some of: transformer models and the Hugging Face ecosystem, Whisper or other ASR, embeddings and semantic search, LLM APIs, fine tuning (LoRA or similar).
** Working depth on the fundamentals underneath: tokenization, attention, evaluation methodology, train and validation discipline. You do not need to derive backprop on a whiteboard, but you should know why your metric moved.
** Comfort building or consuming HTTP APIs and reading what is actually on the wire.
** Good Git and collaboration habits: clean commits, readable pull requests, useful review comments.
** Fluency with AI coding tools, and a considered opinion about where they help and where they hurt.
** You are a genuine cricket fan. Same as everywhere else at CommBox, and it matters twice as much here: you will spend a lot of time judging whether model output about cricket is right, and no metric replaces knowing the game.

Nice to have
** PyTorch training experience and basic GPU literacy: memory, batching, mixed precision, and why the job goes CUDA OOM.
** Speech beyond ASR: TTS, speaker embeddings, source separation, forced alignment.
** Indic language NLP: Hindi and code switched text, Indic tokenizers or models.
** RAG systems, and a vector store in production (Chroma, pgvector, Qdrant or similar).
** Docker, Kubernetes basics, AWS, and CI/CD.
** SQL beyond the basics: joins you trust, indexes you understand.
** Some computer vision curiosity. Several of our problems turn multimodal quickly.
